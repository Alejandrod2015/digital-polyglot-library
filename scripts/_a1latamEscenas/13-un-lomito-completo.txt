Inside an old corner bar in San Telmo, Buenos Aires, at midday. Warm light
through a large street window, dark wood panelling, a marble-topped table, a
tiled floor in black and white, shelves of glasses behind. Exactly two men, both
adults, at mid distance, waist-up.

THE TWO MEN MUST NOT SHARE A FACE. A viewer must tell them apart by the head
alone, with the clothes covered up.

PABLO sits at the table on the left, three-quarter view. Age 32, Argentine,
LEAN, long narrow face, straight nose, thin eyebrows, clean-shaven with NO
moustache and NO beard, skin LIGHT. Hair DARK BROWN, wavy, short at the sides
and fuller on top. He wears a NAVY-BLUE zip jacket over a white t-shirt. Both
hands rest flat on the table on either side of a large plate that has just been
set down in front of him, piled high with a steak sandwich, a fried egg, ham and
tomato spilling out of it. He stares at the plate, mouth open, eyebrows up: this
is not what he ordered.

EL MESERO stands beside the table on the right, leaning in, in the middle of
laying down a knife and a fork on the marble with both hands. Age 45, Argentine,
SHORT and BROAD, a head shorter than Pablo if they stood, wide round face, full
cheeks, heavy black eyebrows and a THICK BLACK MOUSTACHE, no beard, skin MEDIUM

NO grey, NO white. He wears a white shirt with the sleeves rolled and a long
black apron. He is unbothered, mouth closed, already turning away.
