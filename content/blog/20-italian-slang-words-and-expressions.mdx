You can finish an entire Italian course and still freeze the first time a friend says *boh* and shrugs. **Italian slang words** are the layer textbooks skip: short, loud, and used in almost every casual sentence between people under sixty.

Below are 20 **Italian slang words and expressions locals actually use**, grouped by the situations where you will hear them. Each one comes with the literal sense, what it really means, and an example in Italian with the English translation in brackets.

One thing to know before you start: Italian slang is less about rare vocabulary than about intensity. Most of these words exist in the dictionary with a perfectly ordinary meaning, and the slang sense comes from tone, context and how hard the speaker leans on the vowel. *Casino* is a gambling house on paper and chaos in the street. That is good news for a learner, because you are not memorizing a second language, you are learning a second use for words you may already know.

## Reactions You Will Hear Constantly

These are the fillers and reflexes of spoken Italian. Learn them first, because they appear in nearly every conversation.

### 1. Boh

**Literal**: Nothing. It is a sound, not a word.
**Meaning**: "No idea." A verbal shrug, usually with the shoulders to match.
*Dove sono le chiavi? Boh.* (Where are the keys? No idea.)

### 2. Dai!

**Literal**: Give.
**Meaning**: "Come on!" It pushes, encourages, or protests, depending on the tone.
*Dai, andiamo!* (Come on, let's go!)

### 3. Magari

**Literal**: Roughly "maybe".
**Meaning**: Two lives in one word. Alone it means "I wish!"; inside a sentence it means "maybe".
*Vinci la lotteria? Magari!* (Are you winning the lottery? I wish!)

### 4. Meno male

**Literal**: Less bad.
**Meaning**: "Thank goodness." Relief after a near miss.
*Meno male che sei arrivato.* (Thank goodness you made it.)

### 5. Figurati

**Literal**: Picture yourself.
**Meaning**: "Don't mention it" or "no problem." The standard warm reply to thanks.
*Grazie mille! Figurati.* (Thanks so much! Don't mention it.)

### 6. Cavolo!

**Literal**: Cabbage.
**Meaning**: A polite stand-in for a much stronger word. "Darn" or "shoot."
*Cavolo, ho dimenticato il telefono.* (Darn, I forgot my phone.)

## Saying Something Is Great or Awful

### 7. Figo / figa

**Literal**: Untranslatable as slang, and be aware the feminine form is vulgar in other contexts.
**Meaning**: "Cool." Applied to things, plans and people. *Che figo!* is the everyday exclamation.
*Il tuo nuovo appartamento è figo.* (Your new flat is cool.)

### 8. Che figata!

**Literal**: What a cool thing.
**Meaning**: "That's awesome!" Stronger and more enthusiastic than *figo*.
*Vai a Tokyo? Che figata!* (You're going to Tokyo? That's awesome!)

### 9. Spaccare

**Literal**: To break, to smash.
**Meaning**: To be excellent, to rock. Said of a performance, an outfit or a person.
*Il concerto ha spaccato.* (The concert rocked.)

### 10. Che schifo!

**Literal**: What disgust.
**Meaning**: "That's gross!" Used for food, weather, behavior, anything.
*Che schifo questo caffè.* (This coffee is disgusting.)

### 11. Che barba!

**Literal**: What a beard.
**Meaning**: "How boring." A long beard implies a long, dull wait.
*Questa riunione, che barba.* (This meeting is so boring.)

### 12. Che palle!

**Literal**: What balls.
**Meaning**: "What a drag." Crude but extremely common among friends. Keep it out of the office.
*Devo lavorare sabato, che palle.* (I have to work Saturday, what a drag.)

## Describing People and Situations

### 13. Sfigato

**Literal**: Without *figa*, so unlucky.
**Meaning**: A loser, or someone with terrible luck. Playful between friends, insulting to a stranger.
*Che sfigato, ha perso il treno di nuovo.* (Poor guy, he missed the train again.)

### 14. Casino

**Literal**: A gambling house.
**Meaning**: A mess, chaos, or simply "a lot". One of the most useful slang words in Italian.
*Che casino in centro oggi.* (What a mess downtown today.)

### 15. Un sacco

**Literal**: A sack.
**Meaning**: "A lot", "loads". It replaces *molto* in nearly all casual speech.
*Mi piace un sacco.* (I like it a lot.)

### 16. Raga

**Literal**: Short for *ragazzi*, guys.
**Meaning**: "Guys", "folks". How you open a message to a group of friends.
*Raga, che facciamo stasera?* (Guys, what are we doing tonight?)

### 17. Mica

**Literal**: A crumb.
**Meaning**: An emphatic negative, roughly "not at all". It sharpens a denial.
*Non è mica facile.* (It's not easy at all.)

## Words About Everyday Life

### 18. Abbiocco

**Literal**: No English equivalent.
**Meaning**: The heavy drowsiness that hits after a big meal. Italians take it seriously enough to have named it.
*Dopo pranzo mi viene sempre l'abbiocco.* (After lunch I always get sleepy.)

### 19. Beccare

**Literal**: To peck.
**Meaning**: To catch someone, or to run into them by chance. *Ci becchiamo* means "see you around."
*Ti ho beccato!* (Caught you!)

### 20. Sclerare

**Literal**: From a medical root meaning to harden.
**Meaning**: To lose it, to freak out. Used for stress, anger or panic.
*Ho sclerato prima dell'esame.* (I freaked out before the exam.)

## A Word About Regional Slang

Everything on this list works across Italy, which is exactly why it is worth learning first. Beyond it, slang splits sharply by region and you will hear words that a friend two hours away does not use. Rome has *scialla* for "chill" and *daje* as its own emphatic version of *dai*. Milan leans on *balotta* for a group of friends. Naples and Sicily bring in dialect so distinct that other Italians treat it as a separate language rather than an accent.

The practical rule for a learner: use the national slang actively, and learn the regional words passively, so you understand them without adopting them. Borrowed regional slang in the wrong city sounds like an impression rather than fluency, and Italians notice immediately. Once you live somewhere long enough, the local words arrive on their own.

## How to Use Italian Slang Without Sounding Odd

Slang is the fastest way to sound natural and the fastest way to sound strange. Three rules keep you on the right side:

1. **Match the company.** *Che palle* and *sfigato* belong with friends your own age. With a colleague or your partner's parents, stay with *che barba* and *meno male*.
