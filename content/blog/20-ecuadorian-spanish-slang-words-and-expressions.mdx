Ecuadorian Spanish is clear, polite, and quietly full of surprises. **Ecuadorian Spanish slang** carries something no other Latin American country has in the same dose: a layer of everyday Quechua words that locals use without thinking about it. Learn a handful of these and a conversation in Quito, Guayaquil, or Cuenca will feel much closer.

Below are 20 **Ecuadorian Spanish slang words and expressions** only locals use. For each one you get the meaning, an example in context, and the English translation, so you know exactly how and when to say it.

## Ecuadorian Slang for People and Greetings

This is how Ecuadorians refer to each other and get a conversation moving.

### 1. Ñaño / ñaña

**Meaning**: Brother or sister, and by extension a very close friend. It comes from Quechua and it is one of the warmest words in the country.

**Example**: "Mi ñaño llega mañana de Cuenca." ("My brother arrives from Cuenca tomorrow.")

### 2. Guambra

**Meaning**: A kid, a teenager, a young person. Another Quechua word, heard constantly in the sierra.

**Example**: "Ese guambra juega bien." ("That kid plays well.")

### 3. El man / la man

**Meaning**: That guy, that girl. Ecuadorians took the English "man" and gave it a feminine version, which exists almost nowhere else.

**Example**: "La man me dijo que venía." ("She told me she was coming.")

### 4. Chuta

**Meaning**: Damn, oh no, wow. The all purpose Ecuadorian interjection for surprise, annoyance, or mild disaster. Softer versions include *chuzo*.

**Example**: "¡Chuta, me olvidé las llaves!" ("Damn, I forgot the keys!")

### 5. Ele

**Meaning**: Look, there you go, see? Used to point something out or to say "told you so", often as *ele ve*.

**Example**: "Ele, ya te dije que era por acá." ("See, I told you it was this way.")

## Ecuadorian Slang Straight from Quechua

These four react to the body before the brain, and they are pure Ecuador.

### 6. Achachay

**Meaning**: It's freezing! Said with a shiver, the moment the páramo wind hits.

**Example**: "¡Achachay, qué frío en Quito de noche!" ("Brr, it's so cold in Quito at night!")

### 7. Arrarray

**Meaning**: It's burning hot! The exact opposite reflex, for hot plates, hot drinks, and the sun.

**Example**: "¡Arrarray, esa sopa quema!" ("Ouch, that soup is boiling!")

### 8. Atatay

**Meaning**: How disgusting, yuck. For bad smells, bad food, and anything unpleasant.

**Example**: "¡Atatay, esa leche está dañada!" ("Yuck, that milk has gone off!")

### 9. Ayayay

**Meaning**: Ouch, that hurts. The instinctive Ecuadorian cry of pain.

**Example**: "¡Ayayay, me golpeé el dedo!" ("Ouch, I hit my finger!")

## Ecuadorian Slang for Everyday Life

The words that fill an ordinary day, from lunch to the weekend.

### 10. Chuchaqui

**Meaning**: A hangover. The most famous Ecuadorian word of them all, and once again Quechua.

**Example**: "Amanecí con un chuchaqui horrible." ("I woke up with a terrible hangover.")

### 11. Chumado

**Meaning**: Drunk. *Chumarse* is to get drunk, which is usually what leads to number 10.

**Example**: "Llegó bien chumado a la casa." ("He got home really drunk.")

### 12. Farra

**Meaning**: A party, a night out. *Irse de farra* is to go out and make a night of it.

**Example**: "El sábado nos vamos de farra." ("On Saturday we're going out partying.")

### 13. Chiro

**Meaning**: Broke, with no money at all.

**Example**: "No puedo salir, estoy chiro." ("I can't go out, I'm broke.")

### 14. Jama / jamar

**Meaning**: Food, and to eat. *La jama* is the meal, *jamar* is what you do to it.

**Example**: "Vamos a jamar algo rápido." ("Let's go grab something quick to eat.")

### 15. De ley

**Meaning**: Definitely, for sure, no doubt. The standard Ecuadorian way to confirm a plan.

**Example**: "¿Vienes mañana? De ley." ("Are you coming tomorrow? For sure.")

### 16. Fresco

**Meaning**: No worries, it's fine, don't stress. Used to calm someone down or wave off an apology.

**Example**: "Fresco, no pasa nada." ("No worries, it's all good.")

### 17. Chapa

**Meaning**: A police officer. Everyday, informal, and heard everywhere.

**Example**: "Cuidado, ahí viene el chapa." ("Careful, here comes the cop.")

### 18. Cachas?

**Meaning**: Do you get it? Are you following? Tacked onto the end of an explanation. *Cachar* is to understand.

**Example**: "Hay que llegar temprano, ¿cachas?" ("We need to get there early, you know?")

### 19. Chendo

**Meaning**: A joke, a wind up. *De chendo* means "just kidding", and it is especially loved on the coast.

**Example**: "Tranquilo, era de chendo." ("Relax, I was just kidding.")

### 20. Yapa

**Meaning**: A little extra thrown in for free. Ask for *la yapa* at the market and the vendor adds a bit more.

**Example**: "Deme la yapita, casera." ("Throw in a little extra, please.")

## What Makes Ecuadorian Spanish Sound So Different

The slang sits on top of a few habits that make Ecuadorian Spanish one of the friendliest accents to learn from:

- **Quechua is not a museum piece.** *Ñaño*, *guambra*, *chuchaqui*, *achachay*, and *yapa* all come from Quechua and live in ordinary sentences, not in folklore. No other Latin American Spanish leans on it this heavily in daily speech.
- **Politeness runs deep.** Ecuadorians use *usted* far more than their neighbors, even with friends and family, and even alongside the slang on this list.
- **Diminutives soften everything.** *Un ratito*, *la yapita*, *ahicito*. They are not about size, they are about being warm and unhurried.
- **The coast and the sierra sound like two countries.** Guayaquil speaks fast and drops the s, Quito speaks slower and clearer. The vocabulary overlaps, the music does not.
